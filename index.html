      Chapter 7 · Under the Hood

      The Logic Behind the Game

      G.R.O.O.T. runs on just three variables. Meet them one at a time.

      Counting semaphoreempty

      Counts available spaces in the buffer.

      Initial value: 5

      Producer waits here when the buffer is full.

      Counting semaphorefull

      Counts items ready for consumption.

      Initial value: 0

      Consumer waits here when the buffer is empty.

      Binary semaphore / lockmutex

      Protects the critical section.

      Initial value: unlocked / 1

      Only one worker can modify the shared buffer at a time.

      Chapter 7 · Under the Hood

      Twelve lines of pseudocode

      If the Control Room is still running, the highlighted lines follow it live.

      Producer pseudocode

      follows the Control Room
      1. produce(item)// outside critical section
      2. wait(empty)// is there a free slot?
      3. wait(mutex)// enter critical section
      4. add item to buffer// critical section
      5. signal(mutex)// leave critical section
      6. signal(full)// one more item ready

      Consumer pseudocode

      follows the Control Room
      1. wait(full)// is there an item?
      2. wait(mutex)// enter critical section
      3. remove item from buffer// critical section
      4. signal(mutex)// leave critical section
      5. signal(empty)// one more free slot
      6. consume(item)// outside critical section
      Important rule: In a correct system, empty + full always equals buffer capacity.

      Fine print for the curious: while a worker sits between its wait() and its matching signal(), one slot is briefly “in transit”. The Control Room counts it, so empty + full + in-transit = 5 at every instant — and empty + full = 5 whenever both workers are between operations.

      Order matters. Swap the first two waits — wait(mutex) before wait(empty) — and a Producer could lock the door, find the buffer full, and fall asleep holding the key. The Consumer can never get in to free a slot: deadlock.
